# Readability system: signposted, warm editorial lectures

Status: accepted (2026-11-09, owner grill rounds 1–4)

The course-grade lectures (ADR-0003) were accurate and loved, but hard to follow: narrative paragraphs routinely ran 150–250 words, headings were the only signposts in a 6,000-word page, and concepts that are chains or stacks had no visual anchor. The owner reported three pains — walls of text, losing their place mid-lecture, and ideas piling up — and authorised rich styling plus light text surgery, provided the text itself survives.

From this decision, a conforming lecture carries a Section Opener and a Section Recap for every major section, a lecture map that shows the whole lecture on one screen, a Contents Rail that highlights where the reader is, and Concept Anchors only where an idea is a shape (chain, stack, timeline, map). Narrative paragraphs carry one idea each and stay under roughly 90 words; splitting preserves the original wording, and any new signpost copy adds no new technical claims. Blocks form a labelled sign system (Command, Expected output, Checkpoint, Trap, Key idea, Recap) so the eye can tell "do this" from "read this". The visual world is warm editorial — serif display headings, sans body, system fonts only, hand-built inline SVG/CSS diagrams, and a small hand-written script for the rail that the page reads perfectly without. This supersedes the "precision and restraint beat expression" line in `PRODUCT.md` with a sharper rule: **no decoration without direction** — every visual element must help the reader know where they are, what a section means, or what to do next, or it is cut.

Acceptance is the **lost test**: a reader landing mid-lecture knows within ten seconds where they are, what the section teaches, and what to do next. Rollout is pilot-first, as with ADR-0003: Lecture 1 ships first with mechanical checks (paragraph limit, opener/recap coverage, code and links intact, clean Jekyll build) plus desktop and phone screenshots, and the owner personally passes the lost test before the system rolls to the other eleven lectures, then to the home, capstone, setup, and glossary pages. Dark mode joins at rollout, once the colour system has settled; the pilot is light-only but must pass contrast, keyboard, reduced-motion, and print checks. The rejected alternatives were styling alone (cannot fix a 250-word paragraph), a full text rewrite (loses the text the owner loves), and interactive steppers throughout (fights a 40-minute linear read).
